# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Two audiences share one surface. **Players** are curious people — often tourists or students walking through a garden — who want plant discovery to feel like a game rather than a lecture; they arrive on a phone or a laptop, with a few minutes of attention, and want to see their collection and play a quick battle. **Evaluators and partners** (course graders, and the B2B visitors the landing page targets) arrive cold, need to understand what the product does in under a minute, and judge credibility from the interface itself.

The job to be done: turn a real plant you walked past into a creature you own, and make the resulting collection worth coming back to.

## Product Purpose

Sprout is the web platform for a mobile-first nature-gamification product. It identifies a plant from a photograph, renders it as pixel-art creature art, keeps a persistent per-species collection, and lets collected plants battle a server-authoritative opponent. The web app both demonstrates the product to partners and gives existing mobile players a larger screen for their archive.

Success looks like: a visitor understands "scan, grow, battle" without scrolling twice, and a player can get from login to a finished battle without instruction.

## Brand Personality

**Botanical, considered, playful underneath.** The voice is a field naturalist's, not a game marketer's: specific, unhurried, quietly authoritative. Plant names are given properly. Numbers are real. The game lives inside a serious frame — the delight comes from discovering that a herbarium plate is also a battle stat card, not from shouting about fun.

Three words: *specimen, warm, exact.*

## Anti-references

- **Generic game UI**: rounded-sans friendliness, emoji as iconography, chunky pixel chrome applied as decoration. A competing design in this same product space already occupies that lane; matching it forfeits the difference.
- **SaaS marketing template**: hero-metric rows, identical three-card feature grids, tiny uppercase eyebrows above every section, `01 / 02 / 03` numbered scaffolding.
- **Monochrome green**: a single-hue forest palette top to bottom reads as one long undifferentiated surface. Green is the subject, not the entire system.
- **Cream/sand body backgrounds** and gradient text.

## Design Principles

1. **The specimen is the hero.** Every screen is organised around a plant and its record — art, taxonomy, provenance, stats. Chrome serves the specimen; it never competes.
2. **Show the real record.** Real species names, real habitat and conservation data, real stat numbers, real reference codes. Credibility comes from specificity, and the product already has the data.
3. **Serious frame, playful contents.** Field-guide structure — plates, rules, taxonomy labels — carrying game mechanics. The tension is the identity.
4. **Earn every ornament.** A rule, a plate number, or a texture appears because it organises information, not because it decorates. When in doubt, remove it and see what breaks.
5. **Honest states.** Loading, empty, error, and unverified states are designed, not afterthoughts. The product's own documentation is scrupulous about what is and isn't proven; the interface should feel the same.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA. Body text ≥4.5:1, large text ≥3:1, including placeholders and muted metadata — the cream-on-dark palette makes this easy to hit but easy to lose when muting for "elegance". Every animation needs a `prefers-reduced-motion` alternative. Colour is never the sole carrier of meaning: battle outcomes, verification status, and delivery states each pair colour with text or shape. Interactive targets ≥44px on touch. The archive grid must remain operable and readable at 320px.
